Section Two

The application, and why we declined it

We received an application this week. We screened it, and the applicant did not meet our Resident Selection Criteria. We declined it.

We want to address that directly, because we know how it lands.

It is disappointing. We felt it too — we want this home leased just as much as you do, and we do not get paid until it is. Turning down an application is not something we do lightly or without regret.

But this is precisely what you hire us for.

Anyone can accept an application. The value of a professional manager is in the one we decline — the household that looked fine on the surface and did not hold up under verification.

Why we will not take the risk

A vacancy costs you rent. A bad placement can cost you far more than that, and for far longer.

Missed rent while the process runs its course. Legal fees. A vacancy at the end of it anyway. A turnover heavier than a normal one. And damage that routinely exceeds the deposit — on a home of this quality, with a detached apartment and the finishes yours has, that figure is not small.

It is entirely possible for a single bad placement to erase a year of income and leave the property in worse condition than it started. We have seen it. We are not willing to expose you to it in order to fill a vacancy three weeks sooner.

We apply the same written criteria to every applicant, on every property, every time. That consistency is what protects you — and it is also what protects us both under fair housing law. We do not make exceptions because a home has been on the market for a few weeks.

We would far rather tell you we declined an application than tell you six months from now that we placed someone we should not have.

Section Three

What the application actually tells us

There is a genuinely positive signal buried in this week, and it is worth naming.

The price is working. Two weeks ago we had showings and no applications. This week someone toured the home, liked it enough to pay an application fee, and committed to the process. That is a meaningful step forward in the campaign, even though this particular household did not qualify.

A home priced above its market does not generate applications. It generates polite silence. We are past that stage.

What this means for pricing

We are recommending no change this week.

Eight showings, building week over week, and an application in hand tell us the number is landing where it should. Reducing now would give away rent to solve a problem we do not have — the issue was never the price, it was this particular applicant's qualifications.

Section Four

What we are doing next

  • Working every prospect from the last four showings directly. Same-day follow-up where possible, and a second follow-up before any lead is treated as closed.
  • Re-contacting earlier prospects, including the household weighing their family member's plans for the garage apartment and the one who toured before the washer and dryer were added.
  • Continuing to show the home as normal. Nothing pauses because of a declined application.
  • Monitoring competing Laureate Park listings daily for price movement or new inventory.
  • Re-verifying the listing on every portal, logged out, as part of the weekly cycle.

The goal was never simply to get an application. It is to generate enough qualified interest that we can choose a resident rather than accept one. Eight showings in three weeks is what that looks like while it is working.

Your next report arrives Friday morning. In the meantime, Becky will keep you updated directly as showings happen and as feedback comes in.
